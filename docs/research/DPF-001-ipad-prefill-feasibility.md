# DPF-001 — M2 iPad prefill feasibility spike

**Date:** 2026-10-08; direct-cable recheck 2026-10-10\
**Follow-up, 2026-10-11:** The separately authorized [Backburner PoC](DPF-001-ipad-prefill-poc.md) completed app provisioning, transport measurements, and actual split prefill. It observed shorter prefill in the tested cases; its measurements and qualified decision supersede the unknowns below. This original spike remains a record of the evidence available before that experiment.

**Decision:** **Wired transport is feasible; no-go for PulsarMLX implementation on the present evidence.** The intended 12.9-inch iPad Pro (6th generation, M2) now negotiates **10 Gb/s USB** when connected directly to the MacBook. Its app-level transfer rate and actual prefill compute have not been measured. A small end-to-end inference comparison is the next proof, outside this spike's constraints.

## Transport finding

**A usable wired channel exists on this pair.** Backburner demonstrates ordinary TCP sockets over the iPhone/iPad USB network interface, using a link-local `169.254/16` address. Its [device discovery script](https://github.com/StayLameBro/backburner/blob/c03893748386a65c6a7312be39659c9d35026de3/scripts/phone-up.sh) first finds a wired mobile device in IOUSB, then discovers its USB-network address and tests the app's server. Its [iPad-side bridge](https://github.com/StayLameBro/backburner/blob/c03893748386a65c6a7312be39659c9d35026de3/ios/Backburner/Sidecar/RPCBridge.mm) calls this USB NCM and binds the service through a [wired-interface gate](https://github.com/StayLameBro/backburner/blob/c03893748386a65c6a7312be39659c9d35026de3/ios/Backburner/Sidecar/CableOnly.h). The live `en28` link on this Mac reached the iPad by ICMP. This is USB networking, not shared memory or a Thunderbolt PCIe peer. The M2 iPad Pro port supports Thunderbolt 3/USB4 up to **40 Gb/s** and USB 3.1 Gen 2 up to **10 Gb/s**; the **direct connection negotiated the latter**. Backburner's documented setup uses a **10 Gb/s USB-C cable**, but its demo's negotiated link speed is unpublished.

Backburner [reports](https://github.com/StayLameBro/backburner/blob/c03893748386a65c6a7312be39659c9d35026de3/docs/TWO-PHONES.md) **96 µs median / 183 µs p90** for a 16-byte app-level round trip on an A18 Pro iPhone over USB-C. It also estimates about **3 ms for 2.6 MB** on a 10 Gb/s cable in a proposed two-phone pipeline. The repo does **not** provide a macOS USB-tree readout or other verification of the demo's negotiated link speed; **10 Gb/s is the cable rating**, not a confirmed link measurement. The RTT is a third-party measurement on different hardware, and the 3 ms figure is an estimate, not a target-iPad measurement.

**Measured on the target M2 iPad through the hub (Oct 8, 480 Mb/s):** Unicast `ping` over `en28`, 20 packets of 56 data bytes, returned **20/20** with RTT **0.245/0.378/0.804 ms min/mean/max**. Xcode 27's CoreDevice `temporary` file domain copied random synthetic data and verified each round trip by SHA-256: **3 MiB** took **0.896 s Mac→iPad** and **0.875 s iPad→Mac**; **32 MiB** took **2.227 s** and **1.809 s**; **64 MiB** took **3.004 s** and **3.042 s** (**21.3/21.0 MiB/s end-to-end**).

**Measured directly connected (Oct 10, 10 Gb/s):** The same interface returned **20/20** ICMP replies with RTT **0.446/0.833/1.078 ms min/mean/max**. Fresh SHA-256-verified CoreDevice copies of **3 MiB** took **0.282 s Mac→iPad** and **0.246 s iPad→Mac**; **64 MiB** took **1.958 s** (**32.7 MiB/s**) and **0.910 s** (**70.3 MiB/s**), respectively. These are single trials and CoreDevice command/file-service wall times, **not persistent app-socket RTT or throughput**. Both temporary transfer domains were emptied afterward. Backburner's app service ports 50060–50062 were previously closed, so no app-level test was available without iPad-side software. The 10 Gb/s negotiation is a physical-link observation; it does not establish a 10 Gb/s application data rate.

## What Backburner actually does

For Qwen3.8-27B on an M4 Pro Mac and A19 Pro iPhone, Backburner sends each 256-token batch's residual after the Mac's first 40 layers to the phone for layers 41–64 while the Mac starts the next batch. The last batch's tail runs on the Mac so final outputs stay local. The phone holds a tail-layer KV/recurrent-state mirror; the Mac pushes missing prefix state and later merges new tail state from the phone. The current [split implementation](https://github.com/StayLameBro/backburner-llama.cpp/blob/1839b78175d4f89f74023a2ced04f4e558abc4d4/src/llama-split.cpp) sends **f32** residuals by default, because f16 changed its measured draft acceptance, and retrieves tail state at completion. Its [README](https://github.com/StayLameBro/backburner) reports **29–44%** faster 2,000-token reads at 16k–48k existing context on that *specific* Mac/phone/model combination. The iPad app installs on M-series iPads, but the authors [state iPad speed and app memory budget are untested](https://github.com/StayLameBro/backburner/blob/c03893748386a65c6a7312be39659c9d35026de3/docs/INSTALL-IPHONE.md).

The reusable idea is chunked, overlapping layer stages with a small boundary activation and a state mirror. Hardware/model-specific parts include the A19 Pro's GPU neural accelerators (Backburner reports a 2.4× gain for its phone tail), M4 Pro SME2 CPU matmuls, its Qwen hybrid attention/recurrent state, custom Metal kernels, quantization, context policy, and its chosen layer split. Apple says Metal tensor operations [fall back to shader implementations on older GPUs without neural accelerators](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111432/); the M2 iPad cannot be assigned the A19 tail rate. This Mac reports no SME2. Backburner's phone-result percentage is therefore **not transferable** to the M2 Max/M2 iPad pair.

## Compute and transfer estimates — not measurements

**Relative capacity proxies.** The target M2 iPad has 10 GPU cores and **100 GB/s** memory bandwidth; this Mac has 38 GPU cores and the M2 Max's **400 GB/s** bandwidth. Ratios are **0.26 by cores** and **0.25 by bandwidth**. Published Geekbench Metal scores of about **42.8k** for this iPad generation and **140k** for the 38-core M2 Max imply an approximately **0.30** ratio. These are GPU proxies, not prefill benchmarks. Use **r = 0.30** M2-iPad/M2-Max per-layer rate only as an illustrative scenario below; actual Metal kernel, quantization, and thermal behavior could change it substantially. For context, an M1 iPad's published Metal score of about **27.7k** would imply a **0.20** proxy ratio, but that is no longer the target device. [Apple M2 bandwidth](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2022/06/apple-unveils-m2-with-breakthrough-performance-and-capabilities/), [M1 iPad Geekbench](https://browser.geekbench.com/ios_devices/ipad-pro-12-9-inch-5th-generation), [M2 iPad Geekbench](https://browser.geekbench.com/ios_devices/ipad14-5), [M2 Max Geekbench](https://browser.geekbench.com/macs/macbook-pro-16-inch-2023-m2-max-38c-gpu).

**Representative tensor.** Use Meta's [Llama 3.2 3B architecture](https://github.com/meta-llama/llama-models/blob/main/models/sku_list.py): 28 layers, width 3,072, 24 query heads, 8 KV heads (head width 128), and 256-token chunks. A Backburner-style f32 boundary residual is `256 × 3072 × 4 = 3,145,728 bytes = 3 MiB` **Mac → iPad**. New f16 K and V for one tail layer are `256 × 2 × 8 × 128 × 2 = 1 MiB` **iPad → Mac** when the Mac merges the remote state. Thus a 3/4/5-layer tail moves roughly **6/7/8 MiB** per chunk across both directions, before framing, tap rows, and prefix mirroring. At an **assumed**, unmeasured effective 0.5–1.0 GB/s, a 4-layer chunk's 7 MiB costs **7.3–14.7 ms** of aggregate serialization; even an ideal 10 Gb/s link has a **5.9 ms** byte-only floor. A hypothetical 40 Gb/s application link would lower that byte-only floor to **1.5 ms**; it has not been observed on this pair. Whether directions overlap in practice is unknown. A cold 16k-token tail mirror for 4 layers is another **256 MiB** of f16 KV data, potentially hundreds of milliseconds; already-mirrored sessions avoid most of that push. For 2,048 tokens with seven remote chunks and a local final chunk, the 4-layer case would move about **49 MiB** of residual plus new tail KV.

**Direct-link bound.** At the observed **10 Gb/s USB negotiation** (1.25 GB/s raw), the byte-only floor is **2.5 ms for a 3 MiB residual** and **5.9 ms for 7 MiB** of residual plus four-layer KV return, before protocol overhead. This makes an assumed 0.5–1.0 GB/s app data rate physically possible, but it remains **unmeasured**. The CoreDevice file-service results are much slower and cannot be substituted for persistent app-socket throughput. The earlier 480 Mb/s hub path would instead have a **52 ms / 122 ms** floor for those transfers.

**Pipeline math.** Let `T` be this Mac's actual full-model time for one 256-token chunk; `k` of 28 layers run on the iPad at rate `r` relative to the Mac. A simplified two-stage model has `M = T(28-k)/28`, `I = Tk/(28r) + transfer`, steady cycle `max(M,I)`, and `N`-chunk completion `M + I + (N-1)max(M,I)`. It assumes similar layers, stable clocks, and serial aggregate traffic; it omits state merge, app scheduling, initial mirror, and Backburner's local-final-chunk detail. With **zero transfer**, the ideal long-stream upper bound at `r=0.30` is **1.30×**; the best eight-chunk read is **1.14×** with an integer layer split in this simplified pipeline.

For an **illustration only**, set `T=100 ms`, `r=0.30`, and link throughput to the **unmeasured** 0.5 GB/s scenario. This is **not** a predicted Mac or iPad benchmark:

| Tail split | Mac stage | iPad compute + transfer | Steady-state throughput | 2k-token completion vs Mac alone | 4k-token completion vs Mac alone |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3/28 layers | 89.3 ms | 35.7 + 12.6 ms | 2,867 token/s | 1.05× | 1.08× |
| 4/28 layers | 85.7 ms | 47.6 + 14.7 ms | 2,987 token/s | 1.07× | 1.12× |
| 5/28 layers | 82.1 ms | 59.5 + 16.8 ms | 3,117 token/s | 1.09× | 1.15× |
| 6/28 layers | 78.6 ms | 71.4 + 18.9 ms | 2,835 token/s | 1.00× | 1.05× |

The assumed `T` matters: at `T=50 ms`, the best of these splits is roughly **1.05×** for 2k tokens; at `T=200 ms`, roughly **1.10×**. The target M2 iPad lacks a measured layer rate and app-socket throughput. None of these figures include thermal throttling.

For the table's 5-layer, 2k-token case, replacing 0.5 GB/s with a **hypothetical perfect 10 Gb/s (1.25 GB/s) app link** changes the modeled completion from **1.09× to 1.11×** Mac-only. A faster app link helps the transfer term if software can exploit it, but cannot remove the M2 iPad's compute and pipeline fill/drain limits. On the former **480 Mb/s** hub path, even the ideal byte-only time for this 8 MiB/chunk split was about **140 ms**, so the illustrative `T=100 ms` case would regress before protocol overhead. These are sensitivity calculations, not device benchmarks.

**Memory/thermal.** A 4-bit 3B model needs at least ~1.5 GB of weights overall before quantization metadata; a 4-layer tail is roughly a few hundred MB, plus buffers. Llama 3.2 3B f16 KV is **112 KiB/token** for all 28 layers, or **1.75 GiB at 16k** and **3.5 GiB at 32k**; the 4-layer iPad tail holds **256 MiB at 16k** and **512 MiB at 32k**. The bigger unknown is usable iPadOS app memory, especially for Backburner's ~5.1 GB 27B tail. The iPad is fanless and Backburner requires the app in the foreground for GPU work, so sustained rate and usability require a warm-device check. Backburner's guide explicitly says iPad app budget and layer speed have not been tested.

## Decision and smallest proof

1. **Usable wired transport?** **Yes** on the target M2 iPad: the direct connection negotiates **10 Gb/s USB** and a wired link-local interface replies. App-level RTT/throughput remain unmeasured because no iPad app is serving Backburner's protocol.
2. **Enough iPad compute to pay for traffic?** The target M2 iPad looks roughly one-quarter to three-tenths of the M2 Max by public GPU proxies. The faster direct link removes the obvious USB 2 physical bottleneck, but actual layer compute, app transfer, and state-mirror costs remain unknown. Acceleration cannot yet be claimed.
3. **Realistic improvement?** **None measured.** Under explicitly favorable **unmeasured** 0.5 GB/s app-transfer and `r=0.30` compute assumptions, selected illustrative M2 splits yield about **5–10% on a 2k read** and **8–15% on a 4k read**; zero gain or regression remains credible. Backburner's 29–44% is not evidence for this pair.
4. **Smallest next experiment if reconsidered:** use the existing Backburner iPad app and a small supported model for one controlled **same-build Mac-only vs Mac+iPad** 2k/4k prefill comparison. Record app memory budget, app-socket RTT/throughput, per-stage and state-transfer times, wall time, output parity, and a warm-device repeat. This requires cooperating iPad software and model weights in a later experiment; none were installed or downloaded for this spike.
5. **Proceed?** **Stop the research spike here; do not start PulsarMLX runtime implementation yet.** The transport prerequisite passes on the direct cable, but no inference evidence establishes net prefill acceleration. A separate minimal end-to-end benchmark is warranted if pursuing the idea further.

No PulsarMLX runtime files were changed, no inference benchmark was run, and this note is local and uncommitted.
